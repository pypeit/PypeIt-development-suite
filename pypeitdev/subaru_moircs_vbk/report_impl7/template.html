Standards, sensitivity function and the chip-2 transfer

Two exposures of the A2IV star HIP59174, taken through two slits of the science mask on detector 1, give one spliced sensitivity function over 1.90–2.54 µm. Detector 2 has no standard, so its sensitivity is transferred from detector 1 with the ratio of the dome flats of the two channels, checked against the night sky.

Implementation #7 · 2026-10-03 · baseline run5 (reidentify) · one PypeIt change

The standard star

HIP59174 is HD 105388 in SIMBAD: spectral type A2IV, V = 7.45, 2MASS J/H/Ks = 7.343 / 7.39 / 7.359. The sensitivity function takes the V magnitude (star_mag), which PypeIt passes to the stellar model as V_mag. Each set has an A and a B position 15 s apart; the star is the highest-S/N object in all four frames (maxnumber_std = 1, S/N 200–226). A and B were coadded per set.

Sensitivity function

The IR algorithm fits a stellar model, a telluric model and a 13th-order polynomial zeropoint to each standard, then splices the two. Two stellar models and two telluric models were tried. The adopted combination is the Vega spectrum (A0) with the PCA telluric model, now the MOIRCS default.

The stellar model also sets the flux scale. Scaled to V, the Vega spectrum assumes V−K = 0, but this star has V−Ks = 0.09. At 2.13–2.19 µm the Vega model is of the 2MASS Ks flux and the Kurucz A2 model , so the two zeropoints differ by 0.12 mag at every wavelength. The ~7% low scale of the adopted model is within the ~5% A-to-B slit-loss differences of these exposures, but the choice belongs to the instrument scientist.

A 13% step at the splice

In the overlap the two standards disagree by up to 0.135 mag. PypeIt splices by giving the overlap to the redder spectrum, starting at its blue edge, which is exactly where std1 is weakest. The spliced zeropoint therefore drops by 0.14 mag at 2.143 µm.

Consequence: one sensitivity function for all slits carries ~10% slit-to-slit errors near the chip edges. Dividing each slit by its raw flat relative to the chip mean would make std1 and std2 agree to ~2% and remove the step. Not done here: it changes the fluxing model.

The standards' wavelength offset

The standards use the OH solution of the science frames, taken 2.3 h earlier. Their own OH lines sit 0.8–1.2 px lower in the two star slits (Implementation #4), so their features appear ~2 Å to the blue. The IR fit shifts and stretches the telluric model; converted to detector pixels, it absorbs that offset at the blue end of each spectrum.

F2/F1: how much more detector 2 records

The two channels see the same dome lamp. For each science slit, the lamp-on minus lamp-off flat is collapsed along the slit (median per spatial pixel), turned into counts per Å with that detector's wavelength solution, and averaged over slits with a fitted scale per slit. The ratio is smoothed over 200 Å.

Answer: a constant scale is enough to ~3% (1.21 from the flats, 1.25 from the sky). Applying the detector-1 sensitivity function to detector 2 unchanged makes detector-2 fluxes 21–25% too bright.

Transfer and fluxing

transfer_sensfunc.py builds the detector-2 sensitivity function in two modes: direct (the detector-1 zeropoint, times an optional constant scale) and flatratio (plus 2.5 log₁₀ R). Where R is undefined the zeropoint is left empty, and fluxing masks those pixels instead of extrapolating. Each science spec1d is then split into one file per detector, and the standard pypeit_flux_calib pairs each file with its own sensitivity function.

  • pypeit_flux_calib and pypeit_coadd_1dspec accept the split single-detector files, so the in-script fallback (option A) is not needed.
  • The science pair has no object on detector 2 (only DET01 slit 469). The detector-2 test therefore uses the 12 sky-only detections of std1 A. In both copies the flux ratio equals 1/R to 7×10⁻¹⁵, so the mechanics work; a real detector-2 source is still needed to test the transfer against data.
  • The spliced sensitivity function ends at 2.537 µm, so the reddest detector-1 slit (98, to 2.544 µm) loses 37 pixels.

PypeIt changes and problems found

changed MOIRCS telluric model

subaru_moircs.py set the TelFit_MaunaKea grid without teltype = grid, so pypeit_sensfunc stopped for every MOIRCS grism ("Could NOT read the number of PCA components"). The default is now TellPCA_3000_26000_R10000.fits (6 MB, PCA), as for the other near-IR spectrographs. With no overrides it reproduces the adopted zeropoint exactly. This also applies to HK500, which had no working standard processing.

open Splicing spectra of different lengths

SensFunc.unpack_std takes np.max(…, axis=1) over the stacked spectra and fails when they differ in length, as OneSpec coadds do (2024 vs 2021 px). Worked around by coadding both sets onto 2031-px grids.

open Splice rule

The redder spectrum takes the whole overlap from its blue edge, the worst place for a slit spectrum at the chip edge. Splicing at the middle of the overlap, or weighting by S/N, would be gentler.

note Telluric files are moved, not copied

For development versions, a downloaded telluric file is moved from the cache into pypeit/data/telluric/atm_grids/. Two sensfunc runs started together after the 8.3 GB TelFit download raced on the move and one failed. Run one job first.

For the instrument scientist and the team

  • Transfer default: flatratio and a constant scale agree to ~3%; flat and sky differ by 3% in level.
  • Slit throughput: ~10% differences between slits near the chip edges, the cause of the splice step.
  • Stellar model: the Vega spectrum scaled to V (−7% at Ks) or the Kurucz A2 model (+3%, coarse).
  • Telluric stretch: the standards' telluric offset grows from ~1 px to +2.5 px across each spectrum.
  • A detector-2 source: needed to test the transfer on real data.
