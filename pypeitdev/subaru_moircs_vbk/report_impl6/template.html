VB_K wavelength checks

The OH sky-line wavelength solutions of run5 were tested against two references that do not use the OH lines: the Ar lines in the evening ThAr arcs, and the slit positions in the mask image. Both agree with the solutions. The arcs show a shift of about one pixel plus a small rotation between the arc and science pointings. Once that is removed, every slit matches to about 0.1 px.

Implementation #6 · 2026-10-03 · baseline run5 (reidentify) · no PypeIt change

Ar lines in the ThAr arcs

PypeIt has no K-band thorium line list, and the ThAr frames are left untyped in the PypeIt file, so the pipeline makes nothing from them. The check script builds the arc image from the four frames (04:52–04:55 UT) and uses the run5 slit edges, tilts and OH solution. It collapses the central 60% of each slit along the tilts and predicts where the 25 Ar_IR_MOSFIRE lines between 1.8 and 2.5 µm should fall. It then measures each line by its first moment. The ThAr lamp is bright only in parts of the K band, so a slit typically shows 4–11 clean Ar lines.

Both kinds of line are flat-topped: they are images of the ~6 px slit, not Gaussians. PypeIt's line finder fits a Gaussian to the top, which splits a bright ThAr line into two peaks, so the check uses the first moment within ±7 px instead. As a control, the OH lines of each fit are measured the same way in the science sky. They give , so the method adds no offset of its own.

Combining the ThAr frames

The lamp does not light the whole mask in any one frame. The lit region moves across the mask from the first frame to the last. A slit is therefore bright in one or two frames and dark in the others.

For the docs: a user who types these ThAr frames as arc by hand should also set [calibrations][arcframe][process] clip = False. MOIRCS's defaults are not changed, because the arcs are untyped by default and the OH (science) frames are also typed arc.

Offsets against the OH solution

Every science slit on both chips has at least four measured Ar lines. Within a slit the offsets are flat with wavelength, so the shape of each OH solution agrees with the arcs. The offsets are shifts. They are negative on DET01 and positive on DET02.

Shift plus rotation

The slit-to-slit differences are not random. Each slit's offset is a linear function of its position across the chip, so the arc spectra are shifted and slightly rotated relative to the science spectra. After that line is removed, the slits scatter by . The standard stars, taken 2.3 hours after the science frames, showed a similar pattern against the science frames in Implementation #4. On DET02 the two patterns nearly coincide.

The arcs (04:52 UT, header airmass 1.0), the science (06:05 UT, airmass 1.25) and the standards (08:25 UT, airmass 1.2) were all taken at different telescope positions. A shift of about one pixel with a rotation of 0.3–0.6 px per 1000 px between pointings is consistent with flexure. It does not affect the science spectra, which are calibrated on their own OH lines. It does affect the standards (VB_K Q9). DET01 slit 469 lies 0.30 px below its line here and 0.20 px below in the standards.

Slit positions in the mask image

A slit's position along the dispersion direction in the mask shifts its spectrum on the detector. At a fixed detector row, the wavelength should therefore change linearly with the slit's row in the mask image. The slope comes out at at every detector row, while the dispersion along the chip varies from 1.84 to 1.98 Å/px. Moving a slit by one pixel in the mask shifts its spectrum by a constant ~1.96 Å.

Fitting the mask row alone, as specified, leaves residuals of . They follow the slit's spatial position, which is the curvature of the spectra across the field. A quadratic in the spatial position brings them down to . What remains is a constant offset for each slit: along the detector the residuals change by at most .

If a slit's OH solution were wrong, both references would see the error. They do not agree: DET01 slit 758 is 2.35 px off in the mask check but on the line in the arc check. So the mask-image residuals come from the mapping between imaging and grism mode, which the model does not capture. DET01 is worse, and there the traced slits are also shifted more (7–23 px) from the mask apertures.

No slit is flagged. This check catches errors of about 3 px (6 Å) or more in a zero point and about 0.5 px in the shape. That is enough for gross failures such as the leave-one-out slit 758 in Implementation #5 (12–37 Å at its red end); the arcs cover the sub-pixel level.

What this means

  • The run5 OH solutions pass both independent checks. The arcs fix every slit's zero point to ~0.1 px after a flexure term and show no error in the solution shape (≤ 0.04 px per 1000 Å). The mask image confirms the relative zero points of all slits to its ~1 px limit.
  • Flexure between pointings is now seen twice: arcs against science here, and standards against science in Implementation #4. Spectra move by ~1 px and rotate by 0.3–0.6 px per 1000 px. This goes to the instrument scientist (prompt 10).
  • ThAr frames typed by hand need clip = False when combined (prompt 9 docs).
  • The Ar line at 21041.57 Å sits 0.3–0.6 px off the other lines in four DET01 slits, probably blended with a thorium line. Leaving it out moves no slit by more than 0.015 px. A K-band ThAr list should check it.
  • Still open: the archive is built from this mask and needs a second VB_K mask to validate it. Coverage below 1.90 µm is untested.
