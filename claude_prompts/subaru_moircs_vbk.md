  line profiles are distorted, and centroids move by up to 2.7 px

the blue, as expected from the OH offsets measured in Implementation #4
(standard - science = -0.83 to -1.21 px in these slits, i.e. the
standards' features sit ~2 A blueward on the science scale). So the
offset is absorbed by the fit at the blue ends. The growth to the red
(stretch 5-7e-4) is not seen in the OH or ThAr checks (constant per
slit, Implementation #6); cause unknown. Residuals left: derivative-like
+-3-5% in the CO2 bands at 1.95-2.07 um and the deep H2O bands above
2.45 um (low S/N, thermal). The zeropoint is a 13th-order polynomial
under the telluric model, so these hardly affect it; they would matter
for a telluric correction of science spectra (not in this pass).

**F2/F1 (dome flats; `transfer_sensfunc.py`, `flux/flat_ratio_det1_to_det2.png/.txt`).**
- F = `pixelflat_raw` (lamp-on minus lamp-off) of each science slit,
  median over the central 80% of the slit length (per spatial pixel),
  divided by the dispersion of the flat's wave image (identical to the
  WaveCalib solution, max difference 0.000 A). Slits are averaged on a
  2 A grid with a fitted scale per slit (geometric mean 1; slit scales
  0.93-1.05 on DET01, 0.97-1.02 on DET02) to avoid jumps where slits
  start or end; R is smoothed with a 101-point (200 A) running median.
- R = F2/F1 = **1.21**, within +-3% (1.18-1.27) over 1.95-2.50 um and
  1.20-1.23 at 2.10-2.45 um, where >= 10 slits per chip overlap. A plain
  mean over slits changes R by up to 4% at the blue end (1-4 slits).
  Above 2.50 um R rises to 1.43 (1-3 slits, steep red cut-off).
  Defined over 19159-25279 A (both chips' slits, 10 px trimmed at each
  slit end).
- Check against the night sky (`sky_ratio_check.py`, `flux/sky_ratio.png/.txt`):
  the processed OH frames (`Arc_A_0_DET0N.fits`), collapsed the same way,
  give S2/S1 = 1.25 (1.20-1.30) in 100 A bins at 2.03-2.50 um.
  Sky/flat = 1.029 +- 0.011 (47 bins, >= 3 slits per chip): **the shape of
  R agrees to ~1%, its level differs by 3%**. Both channels see the sky
  at the same time through the telescope, so the 3% is likely the dome
  screen illuminating the two halves differently. The rise above 2.50 um
  is in the sky ratio too (1.41 at 2.515 um).
- So a constant scale is enough to ~3% over 1.95-2.50 um (s = 1.21 from
  the flat, 1.25 from the sky); the chip-1 sensfunc applied unchanged
  (s = 1) makes DET02 fluxes ~21-25% too bright.
